This work began with a practical question: how can staff without a technical or legal background make better privacy and digital-tool decisions in everyday work?

I started from what people actually needed to decide, then produced documentation they could use at the point of the decision — a 57-page practical guide — and ran the staff workshop that turned it into everyday practice. It is the clearest example I have of translating complexity for end users.

I wrote a 57-page staff guide translating data-protection requirements into practical, scenario-based guidance. It was legally reviewed, and it became the basis for the staff workshop — documentation and training built as one thing rather than two.

Practical scenario-based decisions

Training built on real situations

The workshop uses realistic situations rather than presenting the requirements only as abstract rules.

The goal is to help someone recognise the decision they need to make at the moment they meet it, which is the same test I apply to any documentation I write.

  • Deciding whether a third-party app is appropriate
  • Recognising when personal information needs additional protection
  • Safer device and account practices
  • Handling information-sharing decisions

What this taught me about documentation

A technically correct answer is only useful if the person reading it can understand and use it.

This work taught me to preserve the underlying requirement exactly while changing the language, examples and level of detail for the audience who has to act on it — a habit I now apply to READMEs, case studies and the limitations sections of my technical projects.
